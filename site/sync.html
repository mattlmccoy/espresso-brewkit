    Your Google client ID

    The id is public by design. A browser cannot keep a secret, so Google secures it with an origin allowlist instead: it only works from the exact web address registered against it. That is why this copy of the site can ship one, and why lifting it from the page buys nobody anything.

    Setting it up, once

    Ten minutes, and only you can do it — the credential belongs to your Google account, not to this project.

    1. Open console.cloud.google.com and make a new project. Call it anything.
    2. Under APIs & Services → Library, find Google Drive API and enable it.
    3. Under OAuth consent screen — newer consoles call the whole section Google Auth Platform — choose External and fill in the app name and your email.
    4. Add yourself under Audience → Test users. Use the same address you will sign in with. This step is not optional and owning the project does not cover it: without it Google refuses with “Access blocked: … has not completed the Google verification process”. Leaving the app unpublished is correct — a test user is exactly what you are.
    5. Add the scope .../auth/drive.appdata. It is the narrowest one there is: it grants access to a hidden folder this app creates, and to nothing else in your Drive.
    6. Under Credentials, create an OAuth client ID of type Web application. Under Authorised JavaScript origins add this site's origin — exactly that, no path and no trailing slash. Ignore Authorised domains on the consent screen: it will not take a github.io address, and an unpublished app has no use for one.
    7. Copy the client ID and paste it on the left. Then Sign in and sync on each device you want to keep in step.
    What this does and does not do

    It merges, it does not overwrite. Every sync pulls, merges and pushes. Shots are unioned by id, so using two devices without syncing in between loses nothing. If the same shot was edited in both places, the later edit wins. Deletions are recorded as tombstones and travel too — without them a union could only ever add, and a shot deleted on the laptop would come straight back from the phone.

    On an iPhone or iPad it is a viewer and a logger. No iOS browser has Web Bluetooth — not Safari, and not Chrome, which is Safari underneath — so a phone cannot stream the scale. It can read your shots and curves, rate a shot, check what is running low, and enter weights by hand. Scale streaming stays on the computer, and that is Apple's decision rather than something this project can work around.

    To watch a pour live, do not use this page. Sync is a pull-merge-push against Drive: an account on both ends, and seconds of latency for a number that moves ten times a second. Watch takes the frames straight off the laptop over your own network instead — no account, no server, nothing leaving the room. Start it from Watch on phone on the Live page.

    It is stored in appDataFolder. A hidden area of your Drive that only this app can see; it will not appear among your files. Removing the app's access from your Google account permissions deletes it.
